In the given paper, we study amorphous to crystalline phase transi-
tions in Ge2Sb2Te5 under the influence of a single femtosecond laser
pulse. Specifically, we aim to define the conditions that should be
created to achieve single-pulse crystallization of this material. Based on
experimental data, we developed a numerical model that distinguishes
the role of the ultra-fast heating/cooling rate for this phase transition.

The model is developed upon the interplay between two kinetic
processes: heat conduction and crystallization. Substrate conductivity
type plays a crucial part only when the crystallization time is longer than
the time taken for heat to reach the substrate. Using definition of the
√̅̅̅̅̅
thermal diffusion length δ =2 αt, we could estimate the characteristic
time of heat diffusion ca. 70 ns through the film of 230 nm thickness. The
characteristic timescale of crystallization according to our simulations
demonstrates approximately the same value, as shown in Fig. 3.
Therefore, when the heat reaches the substrate the phase transition is
already over. Under such conditions the heat conductivity of the sub-
strate is of minimal importance.
Ultimately, the relationship between dynamics of crystallization and
heat conductivity defines the role of substrate in overall process. As we
have chosen a relatively thick film, it happens that in our experiment
this role is insignificant. For thinner films thermal diffusion time be-
comes shorter than the characteristic crystallization time, and the sub-
strate type will be determining.
